The PhD-to-Pharma Resume Checklist

Run your resume through these 12 checks before you send it anywhere. Most academic CVs fail five or more.

12 checks before you apply

  1. It's two pages or less.Industry reviewers spend under a minute on a first pass. Your full publication list goes on LinkedIn or a separate page, not here.
  2. The top third says what job you want.Open with a 2–3 line summary naming the role, e.g. "Cell biologist with 5 years in assay development (flow cytometry, qPCR, ELISA)."
  3. Experience comes before education.Your PhD counts as work experience. List it as a job: title, institution, dates, bullets.
  4. Every bullet starts with a strong verb.Led, developed, validated, reduced, delivered. Never "responsible for" or "worked on."
  5. Most bullets show a result.What changed because of your work? A method others adopted, a timeline shortened, a grant won, a paper published.
  6. There are numbers.Samples run, people trained, % yield improved, budget managed, papers, citations. Aim for a number in half your bullets.
  7. Techniques use industry names.Write "HPLC method development" or "cell-based potency assay," not your lab's nickname for a protocol.
  8. Keywords match the job posting.Copy the exact terms the posting uses (GMP, ICH, CAPA, IND) where they honestly apply. Software screens for them.
  9. Soft skills are shown, not claimed.Replace "strong communicator" with "Presented results to 3 pharma partners" or "Mentored 4 undergraduates."
  10. Collaboration is visible.Industry is team work. Name cross-functional work: core facilities, clinicians, industry collaborators.
  11. Formatting survives software screening.One column, standard headings, no tables or text boxes, saved as .docx or a text-based PDF.
  12. Your LinkedIn headline matches.Recruiters check LinkedIn before calling. Same target role, same keywords, a professional photo.

Keywords by role

Use the ones that truthfully describe your experience.

Regulatory Affairs

IND, NDA, BLA, CTD, Module 3, FDA, ICH, submissions, regulatory strategy, health authority queries

Quality Assurance / Control

GMP, CAPA, deviations, change control, SOPs, batch release, audits, data integrity, 21 CFR Part 11

Analytical Development

Method development, method validation, ICH Q2, HPLC, LC-MS, SEC, stability, specifications, tech transfer

Medical Science Liaison

KOL engagement, scientific exchange, clinical data, therapeutic area, medical affairs, advisory boards

Medical Writing

Clinical study reports, protocols, investigator brochures, regulatory documents, plain-language summaries, AMA style

CMC / Process Development

Scale-up, CDMO management, process characterization, critical quality attributes, control strategy, comparability
