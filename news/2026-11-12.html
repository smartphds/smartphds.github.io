Industry news for PhDs: week of November 12, 2026

U.S. hiring picked up in October and biopharma layoffs remain far below last year, even as a few companies made deep cuts. Here are the stories that matter most for PhDs moving into industry this week.

01 · SHRM · November 6, 2026

U.S. employers add 165,000 jobs in October, led by professional services and health care

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported 165,000 new jobs in October, well above economists' expectations, and unemployment edged down to 4.1%. Professional and business services added 70,000 jobs and health care added 42,000, while August and September were both revised upward. SHRM notes that the average time spent unemployed rose to 27.1 weeks, the longest since September 2021.

What it means for you: Professional and business services, which include consulting, R&D services and technical roles, led October's gains. Job searches are still taking longer than they used to, so start early and keep a steady pipeline of applications.

02 · Fast Company · November 6, 2026

Squarespace, Angi and Block trim staff as tech cuts continue at a slower pace

Squarespace confirmed cuts of about 300 people, roughly 19% of its staff, and Angi disclosed about 300 role eliminations, mostly in middle management and support. Block made an undisclosed number of cuts in customer, risk and content operations teams. Citing Challenger, Gray & Christmas data, Fast Company reports that tech has led all sectors in announced cuts this year with 171,501, though October's tech total of 5,576 was the lowest month of 2026.

What it means for you: Tech is still shedding operations and management roles, so PhDs targeting tech should aim for technical, research and data roles tied to a company's core product, where demand has held up better.

Read the source: Fast Company →

03 · BioSpace · November 12, 2026

Biopharma layoffs rise year over year in October, but 2026 is on track for far fewer cuts

BioSpace counted 15 biotech and pharma companies that made or planned layoffs in October, three more than a year earlier, with affected employees up 43%. Six companies, including Artiva Biotherapeutics, Caribou Biosciences and LENZ Therapeutics, are cutting at least half their staff, and GSK plans to eliminate up to 1,217 roles in Belgium. Even so, the year-to-date total of 99 companies and 19,538 employees is well under half of 2025's full-year figure.

What it means for you: The overall biopharma market is steadier than last year, but small clinical-stage companies remain risky. Before accepting an offer, check a company's cash runway and upcoming trial readouts.

Read the source: BioSpace →

04 · Nature · November 10, 2026

Industry-integrated PhD programs are spreading worldwide

A Nature careers feature describes how countries are redesigning doctorates around industry. In the United States, a new Industry-Integrated PhD backed by $47 million from the National Science Foundation will place more than 250 students in at least one year of on-site industry research, with a goal of finishing in four years. Australia, China and the European Union run similar programs, and an EU study found that 66% of industrial doctorate graduates worked outside academia two years later, compared with 43% from standard programs.

What it means for you: Funders now openly treat industry as the main destination for PhDs. Even if you are in a traditional program, an industry internship or collaboration gives you the same kind of experience these programs are built around.

Read the source: Nature →

05 · AIP FYI · November 9, 2026

Congress returns for a lame-duck session with science funding unresolved

Lawmakers returned on November 9 and must pass funding legislation in December to avoid a government shutdown, according to AIP's science policy newsletter. AIP notes that Senate Appropriations Chair Susan Collins, who has pushed back on some science agency cuts, lost her reelection bid. NIH and NSF have also opened public comment periods on grant administration and on the Survey of Earned Doctorates.

What it means for you: Federal research budgets, and the academic and government jobs they support, remain uncertain into the new year. If your position depends on federal grants, keep an industry search running alongside it.

Read the source: AIP FYI →

06 · Payscale · November 10, 2026

Employers say AI skills will earn more, but few workers have seen raises yet

Payscale's AI Workforce Impact Report finds that 40% of organizations struggle to find candidates with enough AI fluency and 74% expect AI to affect headcount or hiring plans in the next year. Most executives surveyed say their organizations pay or plan to pay more for AI skills, but only 16% of employees say their pay has risen because of AI upskilling. Sixty-one percent of organizations say they are rewriting job descriptions because of AI.

What it means for you: Show concrete examples of using AI tools in your research on your resume and in interviews. Employers say these skills are hard to find, and pay for them has not caught up yet, so salary negotiation is a good place to raise them.
