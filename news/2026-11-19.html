Industry news for PhDs: week of November 19, 2026

New Indeed and Glassdoor research shows employers favoring experienced hires while AI spreads across job postings, and Senate appropriators released science budgets weeks before a December 11 funding deadline. Here is what matters for PhDs heading into industry.

01 · Indeed Hiring Lab · November 18, 2026

AI is showing up in more job postings, but there is little sign of an AI 'jobpocalypse'

Indeed and Glassdoor economists found little evidence that AI alone is causing widespread job losses, describing its impact as broad but still shallow. The share of U.S. postings mentioning AI roughly doubled in a year, from 3.6% to 7.2%, and nearly two-thirds of AI-touched job titles are now outside traditional tech. Software development postings rebounded, but most of the gain was in senior roles, and over a third of the increase mentioned AI in the title.

What it means for you: AI fluency is becoming a baseline expectation well beyond tech, so show concretely how you have used AI tools in your research. The researchers advise building skills that complement AI rather than competing with it.

02 · Indeed Hiring Lab · November 18, 2026

Senior-level postings grow while entry-level openings shrink

As of September 2026, senior-level job postings were 19.2% above January 2025 levels, the only seniority tier that grew, while entry- and mid-level postings each fell about 4%. Entry-level roles now make up a smaller share of all postings than in 2023 and draw several times as many applications per opening as senior roles. Unemployment for recent college graduates reached 5.7% as of June.

What it means for you: Employers are paying for experience, so frame your PhD as years of professional work: projects you led, budgets, collaborations and results. Where you can, target roles that match your seniority rather than defaulting to the crowded entry-level pool.

Read the source: Indeed Hiring Lab →

03 · AIP FYI · November 13, 2026

Senate draft budgets would keep NSF, NASA science and NOAA research near current levels

Senate appropriators released draft FY2027 bills proposing $8.83 billion for NSF, a slight increase, compared with the House's $7 billion, a 20% cut. The Senate would also give NASA science $7.43 billion versus the House's $6 billion and calls for at least 2,000 new NSF Graduate Research Fellowships. Current funding runs out December 11, and AIP reports it is unclear whether Congress will pass full-year bills or extend current funding into the new year.

What it means for you: Federal research budgets remain unsettled, which affects academic hiring, postdoc positions and grant-funded contractors. If you are weighing a postdoc against an industry offer, factor in this uncertainty.

Read the source: AIP FYI →

04 · U.S. Senate Appropriations Committee · November 16, 2026

Senate health bill proposes a $500 million raise for NIH

The Senate Appropriations Committee's draft FY2027 Labor-HHS-Education bill would fund NIH at $49 billion, an increase of $500 million over the FY2026 enacted level, according to the committee's summary. The bill is a draft for committee markup, not enacted law, and must still be reconciled with the House.

What it means for you: A stable or growing NIH budget supports biomedical labs and the postdoc pipeline, but final numbers are not yet set. Keep an industry track active alongside any academic plans until funding is settled.

Read the source: U.S. Senate Appropriations Committee →

05 · BioSpace · November 17, 2026

Cellectis plans to cut up to 70% of staff as AstraZeneca trims U.S. commercial teams

Gene-editing company Cellectis may cut up to 70% of its global workforce, about 160 people, as it shifts from cell therapies to in vivo gene editing, with cuts running into 2027. BioSpace, citing Bloomberg, reports that AstraZeneca cut about 600 jobs in U.S. respiratory and immunology commercial teams as part of a $1.5 billion savings program. Coherus Oncology, Capricor and Milestone Pharmaceuticals also announced reductions this month.

What it means for you: Biotech cuts continue to cluster around strategic pivots and cost programs. When you evaluate an employer, look at its pipeline focus and cash runway, and expect more experienced scientists in the applicant pool for R&D roles.

Read the source: BioSpace →

06 · KOIN 6 News via Yahoo Finance · November 18, 2026

Intel confirms another round of layoffs, this time in IT

Intel confirmed it laid off workers from its IT department this week but would not say how many people were affected or where. The company said the cuts are part of a strategy to be more focused and efficient. Intel cut more than 600 Oregon employees last November and over 2,000 jobs in July 2025.

What it means for you: Large semiconductor employers are still restructuring, so PhDs targeting chip roles should watch for growth areas within companies rather than assume steady hiring across the board.
