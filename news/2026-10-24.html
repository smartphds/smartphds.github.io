Industry news · October 24, 2026

Industry news for PhDs: week of October 24, 2026

Layoffs are cooling, recruiters are more upbeat, and AI skills are becoming a hiring advantage. Here's what moved this month and what it means for your job search.

01 · BioSpace · October 8, 2026

Biopharma layoffs fall to their lowest level since tracking began

BioSpace counted 27 biopharma companies and 2,568 employees affected by layoffs in the third quarter of 2026, its lowest quarterly figures since it began tracking in 2024. Companies making cuts fell 56% and affected employees fell 90% compared with a year earlier. New Jersey and Massachusetts still saw the most cuts, mostly at large pharma companies.

What it means for you: The market is steadier than in 2024 and 2025, which means fewer experienced scientists competing with you for the same roles. Large companies are still trimming in some sites, so check recent news before applying to a specific location.

Read the source: BioSpace →

02 · Fierce Biotech · October 1, 2026

Recruiters see signs of a hiring recovery, with growth beyond Boston and San Francisco

A science recruiting leader at Kelly told Fierce Biotech the firm is seeing signs of recovery and an upward trajectory for 2026. Demand remains strong in California and Massachusetts, with growth in Texas, North Carolina and the Midwest. Fierce logged 21 layoff announcements in Q3, down from 33 in Q1.

What it means for you: Widen your search beyond the two biggest hubs. Texas, North Carolina and the Midwest are worth a look, especially if you can relocate.

Read the source: Fierce Biotech →

03 · Fierce Biotech · October 1, 2026

Employers want scientists who also understand AI

The same recruiter said technical positions are not disappearing because of AI. Instead, hiring managers increasingly want AI knowledge alongside a scientific or clinical discipline, and titles such as AI drug discovery scientist are becoming common.

What it means for you: You don't need to switch fields. Show the data and coding work you already do, and add one practical skill such as Python for data analysis.

Read the source: Fierce Biotech →

04 · IntuitionLabs · August 12, 2026

Big pharma keeps building dedicated AI teams

A review of public announcements found major drugmakers investing in AI groups: Sanofi plans 50 AI and machine learning roles at its Toronto hub by 2028, Boehringer Ingelheim opened a London AI center in April 2026, and Eli Lilly and Nvidia launched a joint AI lab. Common practitioner titles include computational biologist, AI scientist and machine learning engineer.

What it means for you: Computational and hybrid science roles are a growing door into industry. Search those titles alongside traditional scientist roles.

Read the source: IntuitionLabs →

05 · Applied Clinical Trials · January 29, 2026

FDA and EMA principles on AI keep shaping regulatory and quality jobs

In January the FDA and EMA released ten shared principles for using AI across the medicines lifecycle, covering risk-based validation, a defined context of use, data governance and ongoing monitoring. Both agencies expect more detailed guidance to follow.

What it means for you: Regulatory Affairs, Quality and Analytical teams need people who can explain how an AI tool was validated. If you have method validation or QC experience, highlight it.

Read the source: Applied Clinical Trials →

06 · Associated Press via Fortune · October 19, 2026

Academic pressures are pushing more early-career scientists to rethink their path

The AP reports that federal research grant cuts and freezes have pushed some US scientists toward jobs abroad, while many others are staying in the US or moving to industry. It cites Association of American Universities data showing major research institutions accepted 15% fewer applicants for fall 2026 doctoral programs than the year before.

What it means for you: Expect more PhDs and postdocs to look at industry in the coming year. Starting your search early and sharpening your resume now will help you stand out.
