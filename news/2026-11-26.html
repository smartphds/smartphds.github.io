Industry news · November 26, 2026

Industry news for PhDs: week of November 26, 2026

This Thanksgiving week brings an upbeat employer hiring survey, new data on falling international graduate enrollment, a court win for international students and scholars, and a December 11 funding deadline that research employers are watching closely.

01 · ZipRecruiter Economic Research · November 23, 2026

Two-thirds of U.S. employers plan to hire more next year, and more of them now credit AI

ZipRecruiter's annual survey of more than 1,500 hiring managers and recruiters found 67% expect to increase hiring over the next year, up from 63% in 2025. Thirty percent now expect AI to increase their headcount, versus 21% who expect it to shrink it, a reversal from last year. Pay growth is cooling: 59% plan raises, a three-year low. Employers also report a median of 50 applications per opening and say critical thinking is the skill candidates most often lack.

What it means for you: The survey says 41% of employers pay a premium for AI skills and 48% now use skills tests, so be ready to show what you can do, not just list it. Critical thinking is a PhD strength; give concrete examples of it in applications and interviews.

02 · STAT · November 25, 2026

New data show a sharp drop in international graduate students, especially in biomedical PhDs

STAT reports that an Institute of International Education survey of more than 800 institutions found foreign student enrollment down 9% this academic year, with a 15% drop among new and graduate students. An AAMC poll of 37 member institutions found international students starting biomedical PhD programs fell about 30% since 2024. A DHS spokesperson disputed the IIE figures, calling them misleading.

What it means for you: Smaller incoming cohorts can change lab staffing and the future talent pipeline that industry R&D recruits from. International PhDs should keep a close eye on visa and work-authorization rules when planning a move into industry.

03 · AIP FYI · November 18, 2026

Federal judge strikes down the four-year cap on student and scholar visas

On November 16, U.S. District Judge F. Dennis Saylor set aside in full a Department of Homeland Security rule that would have capped F-1 and J-1 admission periods at four years, replacing the long-standing 'duration of status' policy. The judge found DHS exceeded its authority and acted arbitrarily. AIP notes the government may appeal, and cites its own analysis that about 97% of international physics and astronomy PhDs take more than four years to finish.

What it means for you: For now, international PhD students and postdocs keep the flexibility to stay for the length of their program. Rules can still change on appeal, so check with your international office before timing a job search or OPT application.

04 · The Boston Globe (from The New York Times) · November 18, 2026

Senate committee chair blocks the nominee to lead the National Science Foundation

Sen. Bill Cassidy, chair of the Senate health committee, canceled a scheduled vote on Jim O'Neill, the administration's pick to lead NSF, saying O'Neill 'did not do enough to stand up for science.' A White House official said the nomination had not been withdrawn. The report notes NSF has had no Senate-confirmed director since April 2025.

What it means for you: Continued leadership uncertainty at NSF adds to an unsettled outlook for federally funded research positions. If you are weighing academic versus industry paths, factor in how long that uncertainty may last.

05 · AIP FYI · November 23, 2026

Most federal science funding expires December 11, and a grants-rule fight is unresolved

AIP reports that most federal funding runs out on December 11, and that Democrats are reportedly weighing a stopgap into March or April. The Senate's draft spending bills do not extend a current block on an OMB grants rule that would give political appointees more control over awards. Research!America's CEO called that omission 'deeply troubling.'

What it means for you: A short-term funding patch would extend uncertainty for grant-funded jobs into 2027. Postdocs on soft money may want to keep industry options moving in parallel rather than waiting for the outcome.

Read the source: AIP FYI →

06 · TheStreet · November 24, 2026

Procter & Gamble plans deep supply-chain workforce cuts as automation expands

TheStreet reports that P&G reaffirmed at its November 19 investor day a plan to reduce its product-supply workforce, including contractors, by 20% to 30% by 2030. The effort rests on automation, including unattended operations at more than 15 sites, and is part of up to $10 billion in productivity savings targeted for 2027 to 2030. P&G has not said how many people will be affected.

What it means for you: As consumer-goods makers automate, demand shifts toward people who design, model and run automated systems. PhDs in engineering, data science or operations research can position themselves for that work.

Read the source: TheStreet →

07 · BioSpace · November 23, 2026

Novo Nordisk adds 215 New Jersey layoffs as smaller biotechs keep cutting

BioSpace's tracker reports that Novo Nordisk filed a notice to lay off 215 people at its Plainsboro, New Jersey, headquarters, tied to a redesign of its U.S. sales organization, bringing the two recent rounds there to 323. nChroma Bio cut about half its staff after stopping its only clinical trial, and Calidi Biotherapeutics cut about 67% of its workforce to extend its cash runway.

What it means for you: Recent biopharma cuts are concentrated in commercial teams and small, cash-strapped biotechs. When evaluating a startup offer, ask about cash runway and pipeline depth, not just the science.
