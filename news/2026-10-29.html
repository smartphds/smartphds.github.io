Industry news for PhDs: week of October 29, 2026

This week brought a large voluntary buyout at Microsoft, another run of biotech restructurings and fresh hiring data showing where demand is holding up. We also cover quantum computing job growth and proposed visa and grant changes that matter for international and research-funded PhDs.

01 · OPB / KUOW · October 27, 2026

Microsoft offers voluntary buyouts to about a quarter of its workforce

Microsoft is offering voluntary buyouts to roughly 60,000 employees in the U.S., U.K., Canada and India, according to a memo sent to staff on October 26. Eligibility runs up to the vice president level, and some groups, including sales and parts of engineering and marketing, are not included. The package is described as similar to the company's standard severance. A company spokesperson said AI is not driving the program, while the report notes Microsoft has kept trimming staff as it spends billions on AI infrastructure.

What it means for you: Big tech is still reshaping headcount even while it invests heavily, so expect more experienced candidates in the market for technical roles. If you are targeting large tech firms, look closely at the teams that are being protected or growing, such as AI infrastructure, rather than the company as a whole.

02 · BioSpace · October 28, 2026

Another wave of biopharma restructurings, from small biotechs to GSK and Pfizer

BioSpace's layoff tracker logged a busy stretch from October 20 to 28. EyePoint is cutting about 53% of staff (105 people) after a late-stage eye disease trial fell short, and Artiva Biotherapeutics is cutting about 94% of its workforce after ending work on its lead candidate. Tango Therapeutics is trimming about 14% as it narrows its R&D focus, while GSK, Pfizer and Teva announced restructurings tied to cost-cutting and manufacturing changes.

What it means for you: Most of these cuts follow a single trial result or a pipeline reprioritization, which is a reminder to check a small biotech's cash runway and clinical calendar before you accept an offer. Platform and discovery roles are often the first to be cut when a company narrows to one program.

03 · Indeed Hiring Lab · October 22, 2026

Engineering postings stay strong while software development lags

Indeed's October snapshot puts its overall job postings index 2.8% above a year earlier, with unemployment at 4.2% and about one opening per unemployed worker. Engineering postings are among the strongest areas, while software development postings sit well below their pre-pandemic baseline and the Seattle and San Francisco metros rank among the weakest. Data center postings have risen about 20% over three months, and AI-related jobs now make up 7.3% of all postings, more than double the previous peak.

What it means for you: If your training is in physics, materials, electrical or mechanical engineering, demand in engineering and data center buildout is a real opening. For computational PhDs, roles that mention AI are a growing share of postings, so show concrete AI or machine learning work rather than general coding.

04 · Coresignal (press release) · October 27, 2026

Quantum computing job postings in 2026 already top all of last year

An analysis by data provider Coresignal counted 4,518 quantum-related job postings from January through August 2026, about 58% more than in all of 2025. Google, IBM and Amazon Web Services posted the largest shares, and hiring clusters in the Bay Area, Boston and the Washington, D.C. region. Most postings are mid-to-senior level, covering hardware and systems engineering, quantum software, research science, sensing and cryptography. The company notes its data leaves out many company career sites and most hiring in China.

What it means for you: Quantum remains a small field, but it is growing fast and leans toward experienced, research-heavy candidates, which suits PhDs in physics, electrical engineering, computer science and chemistry. Hardware and systems roles are a strong fit if you have lab, cryogenics, optics or device experience.

05 · AIP FYI · October 19, 2026

Proposed rules would tighten H-1B extensions and end a 60-day grace period

AIP's science policy newsletter reports two Department of Homeland Security proposals open for comment. One, published October 14, would stop giving automatic weight to earlier approvals when employers ask to extend H-1B, L-1 and other work visas, shifting the burden to the employer; DHS says the current practice risks undermining program integrity, and comments are due November 13. A separate proposal would remove the discretionary 60-day grace period that lets H-1B and other visa holders stay after a job ends, with comments due November 10.

What it means for you: International PhDs on work visas, or planning to move onto one, should watch these rules closely and talk with an immigration attorney or their employer's counsel about timing. If the grace period goes away, having a backup plan before a role ends becomes much more important.

06 · AIP FYI · October 26, 2026

NASA skips its main annual research solicitation this year, and NSF leaders plan to leave

According to AIP's weekly newsletter, NASA will not release its main annual research funding call, known as ROSES, this year and plans the 2027 call for January; grantees have also reported long delays in receiving promised funds. The same edition reports that top officials at the National Science Foundation plan to leave by the end of 2026. Congress is in recess until November 9.

What it means for you: Gaps and delays in federal research funding make soft-money postdoc and research positions less predictable, especially in space and Earth science. If your current position depends on a pending grant, it is a good time to start building an industry plan alongside it.
