Industry news · November 5, 2026

Industry news for PhDs: week of November 5, 2026

This week brought a calmer layoff picture overall, sharp cuts at a few tech and biotech companies, and an election that changes who controls science budgets. We also look at fresh survey data on how AI is reshaping hiring, from resume screening to quant finance.

01 · Challenger, Gray & Christmas · November 5, 2026

U.S. layoff announcements drop to their lowest October level since 2023

U.S. employers announced 43,994 job cuts in October, down 71% from October 2025, according to the outplacement firm's monthly report. Technology cuts fell to 5,576, the sector's lowest month this year, though tech is still up 21% for the year at about 171,500. Cuts attributed to AI were 1,265 in October, also a 2026 low, and market and economic conditions have now passed AI as the top stated reason for the year. Announced hiring plans rose 57% from a year earlier.

What it means for you: The broad market is steadier than the headlines of earlier this year suggested, which is good news for anyone starting a search now. Tech remains the sector with the most cuts in 2026, so keep a mix of industries on your target list.

02 · TechCrunch · November 4, 2026

Canva cuts about 22% of its workforce as growth slows

The design software company is laying off 1,271 employees, about 22% of staff, after its CEO told employees growth had slowed for the first time in a decade. TechCrunch reports Canva lowered its revenue growth forecast from 30% to 20%, citing high AI costs, and faces competition from AI startups and general-purpose chatbots. The company is merging its product, growth and platform teams and reducing narrow specialization, so remaining staff may take on broader roles.

What it means for you: Even well-known software companies are reorganizing around AI costs and competition. When you interview in tech, expect questions about how you work across functions, and be ready to show breadth alongside your specialty.

Read the source: TechCrunch →

03 · BioSpace · November 4, 2026

Ultragenyx, LENZ and Molecular Partners lead a new round of biotech cuts

BioSpace's layoff tracker reports that Ultragenyx is cutting about 20% of its workforce, to roughly 1,000 employees, as it moves to a smaller, more targeted R&D footprint after a failed Phase 3 trial. In the same stretch, LENZ Therapeutics is reducing headcount by about 75% in a reorganization focused on consumer marketing, and Molecular Partners is cutting up to 44% of staff to focus on its radiopharmaceutical pipeline. Vistagen and Immutep also announced smaller reductions.

What it means for you: Cuts that follow a trial readout tend to land hardest on research and early-stage teams. If you are weighing a biotech offer, ask how many programs are funded, when the next readouts are and how long the cash runway lasts.

Read the source: BioSpace →

04 · STAT · November 4, 2026

Democrats win control of Congress, shifting who oversees science budgets

STAT reports that Democrats gained control of both the House and Senate in the November 3 midterms. The outlet notes that Sen. Susan Collins, a prominent backer of federal research funding, lost her seat, and that the new majorities will have more tools to investigate and push back on the administration's health agenda. The election-night report does not detail specific changes for NIH or other science agencies, and the new Congress does not take office until January.

What it means for you: Policy shifts take months, so the election does not change the funding picture for current postdocs right away. Keep an eye on the next appropriations deadline and on any oversight of grant terminations, which could affect academic and government research jobs in 2027.

Read the source: STAT →

05 · AIP FYI · November 2, 2026

Congress faces a December 12 funding deadline as NSF scales back proposed grant changes

AIP's science policy newsletter reports that most federal funding expires December 12, so Congress must pass spending bills when it returns November 9 or face a shutdown. It also reports that NSF revised its proposed grant policy changes after more than 3,000 public comments, dropping language on ending fixed-price awards, disallowing publication costs and making indirect-cost recovery depend on available funds. The revised proposal is under White House review and would apply only to new awards or new funding.

What it means for you: Another possible shutdown would again slow grants and federal hiring, so researchers on federal funding should plan for delays. The NSF revisions suggest public comments can shape grant rules, which is worth knowing if you work in research administration or science policy.

Read the source: AIP FYI →

06 · Northeastern Global News · November 2, 2026

Survey of 8,000 hiring decision-makers shows AI is changing who gets hired

A Northeastern University survey of 8,174 people who have a say in hiring found that 54% use AI to review resumes, rising to 83% in tech. About half said AI made a difference in their hiring decisions, and 26% said they are hiring fewer entry-level candidates. Almost half said AI experience is not explicitly part of their hiring criteria, though IT and finance look for it more often than other fields.

What it means for you: Your resume is increasingly read by software before a person sees it, so use the exact terms from the job posting and a clean format. Because many PhDs enter industry at the first professional level, frame your research years as work experience with concrete results.

Read the source: Northeastern Global News →

07 · FinTech Global · October 29, 2026

London quant hiring managers say AI and coding skills can outweigh a finance background

Research commissioned by Bloomberg polled 500 London financial services professionals. Among those who make final hiring decisions, 94% said they would hire someone without a conventional finance or quant background who showed strong AI and coding ability, and 54% said they already had. Respondents were split on how much else matters: 48% named AI fluency as a requirement for entering quant investing, 39% named a degree in maths, physics, engineering or computer science, and 13% said AI skills are now expected on top of technical ones.

What it means for you: Quant finance remains open to PhDs from outside economics, especially in physics, math and engineering, but coding and AI fluency are becoming the entry ticket. A portfolio of Python projects or a data competition result can carry real weight in these interviews.
